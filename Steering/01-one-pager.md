# One-Page Pitch

**Working title:** TBD

## Fantasy
A single fictional city in a fictional Western European setting, contemporary real-world technology, no fantasy or supernatural elements. Civilisation has recently collapsed. You begin in the tail era of the apocalypse as the leader of a scav crew and can, over the course of a playthrough, rebuild toward a functioning, "civilised" state — which then carries its own internal risk of collapsing back into faction warfare.

## Core loop
**Survive → Organise → Diplomacy → Scale → Compete**, expressed mechanically as one repeating pattern: fund a venture (an expedition, a raid, a trade run, an act of agitation, a diplomatic gambit) that carries a success chance shaped by who you send, their traits, your faction's traits, and your tech/policy choices, and resolve into an outcome that feeds back into those same traits.

## Comparable games
- **Crusader Kings II** — mortal characters, dynasties, succession, portraits
- **Hearts of Iron IV** — faction identity, off-map pressure, weighted AI
- **Mount & Blade: Bannerlord** — gang-to-kingdom escalation
- **EU4** — lite trade and decision-with-odds resolution
- **Kenshi** — nearest solo-dev precedent for scope and tone

## Audience
Grand strategy / Paradox-adjacent players. This audience is loyal, tolerant of modest production values, and rewards depth and replayability over art fidelity — but benchmarks against thousands of hours of existing genre depth. Compete on being sharp, replayable in a few hours per run, and a strong generator of emergent, clip-worthy stories, rather than on breadth of content.

## Why solo-viable
2D, UI-heavy, data-driven. No 3D open world, no real-time combat, no hand-authored branching content trees. The city is district-based (not block-based), the regional map is nodes and stats (not simulated cities), and faction "personality" is generated by a shared trait engine rather than authored per-faction — so most of the game's apparent depth comes from a small number of interlocking systems, not from volume of hand-written content.
